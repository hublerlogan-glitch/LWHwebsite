How I Measured My Growth (Professional Evolution Analysis)

Public accounting firms need people who are accurate, dependable, easy to work with, and who speak up when something is off. I tested myself on all of that this semester using my Self-Perception Inventory (SPI), two rounds of 360 teammate feedback, peer review surveys (PRS), and the Charisma Diagnostic.


Domain 1: Core Values — Structure and Standards Drive My Work

[ Insert Chart: Structure & Achievement Motivators ]

Claim: My core professional identity is grounded in structural accuracy and high standards of achievement.

Evidence: Stability is my top motivator on the SPI (80/100), followed by achievement (60) and tradition (55). Peers independently verified this identity: all 7 words I selected for myself were also chosen by peers (e.g., Driven picked by 3/4, Rule-Follower and Good with Numbers by 2/4). In Round 2 360 evaluations, teammates rated my quality of work 95.8 vs. the team average of 86.83.

Reasoning: Public accounting relies on compliance, accuracy, and standardized frameworks. My identity and reputation align strongly here, providing a trusted foundation for audit and financial analysis.

Domain 2: Communication Style — A Driver by Nature, Seen as Analytical

[ Insert Chart: Proactivity & Communication Gaps ]

Claim: While my internal orientation is proactive and direct, my initial interpersonal presentation is perceived as reserved and analytical.

Evidence: My self-assessment identifies as a Driver style (proactivity 1.0, reactivity -4.0), whereas 3 of 4 peers rated my outward behavior as Analytical (average proactivity -4.0). Qualitative feedback noted I can be "shy at first, then fully attentive once engaged."

Reasoning & Brand Strategy (Intention vs. Impact): My intention to keep team dynamics positive led me to withhold critical feedback. The impact was that teammates missed out on constructive insights. To reconcile this, my "Warm & Specific" Strategy focuses on three tactical habits:

  • 1. Bring one specific actionable suggestion to every team meeting.
  • 2. Name execution gaps early (specifying what, why, and deadline).
  • 3. Follow up individually to ensure feedback is supportive and clear.

Domain 3: Course Insight — Warmth & Competence Matrix

[ Insert Chart: Charisma Profile & Accountability Trend ]

Claim: I consistently project high warmth and technical competence, with direct accountability serving as my primary growth boundary.

Evidence: My Charisma Profile places me in the Charismatic Zone (Warmth ~5.0, Confidence ~1.8). 360 scores show team environment and effort ratings rising to 99.4 and 99.6 in Round 2, with 5 of 5 teammates noting I contributed more than others. However, holding others accountable dropped from 3.8 to 3.2 between rounds.

Reasoning: During Presentation 3, when out-of-town constraints threatened coordination, I maintained high warmth and effort via virtual calls. However, developing true leadership competence in public accounting requires pairing that warmth with clear accountability standards.
